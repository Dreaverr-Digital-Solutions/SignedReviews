## Why Ecommerce Stores Are Leaving Trustpilot

Trustpilot is the biggest name in online reviews, with [400 million reviews on trustpilot.com](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/). But for ecommerce stores, the platform has three problems.

**Pricing that doesn't fit ecommerce.** Trustpilot's free plan includes 50 automated invitations a month. Paid plans start at $99/month (Starter, only for new small businesses up to $5M revenue) and $319/month (Plus), billed annually on a 12-month contract ([Trustpilot pricing](https://business.trustpilot.com/pricing)). When Judge.me offers unlimited reviews for free and Signed Reviews starts at $29/month, the value proposition gets harder to justify.

**Anyone can review without buying.** [Trustpilot says](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/trust/how-trustpilot-works) consumers can leave a review "with or without an invitation." Its verified reviews come from business-sent invitations linked to a customer interaction, not from a payment processor confirming a purchase. For ecommerce stores, this opens the door to competitors leaving fake negative reviews, customers reviewing products they never bought, and review ganging from forum campaigns. The [FTC's final rule on fake reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), announced in August 2024, makes this a compliance risk, not just a trust problem.

**Product reviews cost extra.** Trustpilot's pricing page lists eCommerce integrations on every plan, so sending invitations after an order is not the problem. Product reviews are: they are sold as a separate paid Product Reviews add-on. Many stores end up paying for Trustpilot plus a Shopify review app for product pages.

## What to Look for in Trustpilot Alternatives for Ecommerce

Not all review platforms are built for online stores. Here's what matters when evaluating trustpilot alternatives for ecommerce:

**Purchase verification.** Can the platform prove a reviewer actually bought the product? Look beyond "Verified Buyer" badges: ask who did the verifying. Platforms that verify against payment processor data (Stripe) tie each review to a payment record. Platforms that verify against store order data (Shopify) are solid but the data source is your own admin. Platforms with no verification at all leave you exposed to fake reviews.

**Shopify, WooCommerce, and store integrations.** Your review platform should integrate natively with your ecommerce stack: auto-sending review invitations after fulfillment, syncing product catalogs, and embedding review widgets on product pages without custom development. If you're paying for a platform, it should work where your customers shop.

**Google rich snippets.** Star ratings in search results directly impact click-through rates. Your review platform needs to generate valid aggregateRating schema markup that Google can parse. Not all platforms do this correctly: some use review counts that don't match the visible page, which Google flags as spam.

**Pricing that scales with you.** Avoid platforms that charge per review or have opaque enterprise pricing. Fixed monthly tiers with clear review volume caps let you budget predictably.

## The 7 Best Trustpilot Alternatives for Ecommerce in 2025

### 1. Signed Reviews. Best for Stripe-Verified Stores

Signed Reviews verifies purchases against Stripe payment data rather than your store's own order records. When Signed Reviews picks up a completed Stripe charge, it sends the customer a review invitation, immediately or after a delay you set. If a charge is refunded in full or disputed, the associated review hides automatically. Each review carries a tamper-evident signature over its content and charge reference, so any later edit is detectable, giving you the strongest "Verified Buyer" signal available. Pricing starts at $29/month with full automation, and the free plan includes unlimited self-service reviews plus 10 free automated review invitations. The honest limitation: Signed Reviews requires Stripe as your payment processor, so stores running exclusively on non-Stripe gateways like Braintree or Authorize.net can't use it.

→ [See pricing](/pricing/) · [How Stripe verification works](/trust/) · [Signed Reviews vs Trustpilot](/vs/trustpilot/)

### 2. Judge.me. Best for Shopify-First Stores

Judge.me is a widely used Shopify review app that reports [142M+ verified reviews collected](https://judge.me/). Its free plan includes unlimited review requests and customizable review displays. Verification works by matching reviews to Shopify order data, strong but sourced from your own store admin rather than an independent processor. The [$15/month paid plan](https://judge.me/pricing) adds AI features, social media and Google Shopping, referrals and coupons, and all integrations; Judge.me says $15 is the most you can ever pay. The limitation: Judge.me is Shopify-only, so if you ever migrate platforms or run a headless storefront, you'll need to rebuild your review stack.

→ [Signed Reviews vs Judge.me](/vs/judge-me/)

### 3. Loox. Best for Budget Shopify Photo Reviews

Loox is a Shopify review app built around automatic photo-review collection. Its [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/loox) shows a free plan for up to 500 orders, with review request emails, photo-review discounts and Google rich snippets. Paid plans start at $49.99/month for 300 orders, plus $50 per additional 300 orders. It verifies against Shopify order data and follows up with customers for photo reviews. The limitation: Loox is Shopify-only with no cross-platform support, and video reviews and API access sit on the paid plan. Photo reviews are its core strength; if you don't need visual UGC, Judge.me's free plan covers the basics.

→ [Signed Reviews vs Loox](/vs/loox/)

### 4. Yotpo. Best for Enterprise DTC

Yotpo is the enterprise DTC default, used by brands generating millions in revenue who need a full customer retention suite alongside reviews. Its "Verified Buyer" badge goes to customers who review a product they bought, matched using the order information Yotpo receives from your ecommerce platform ([Yotpo's badge docs](https://support.yotpo.com/docs/reviewer-badges)). That is a solid Level 3 approach. Yotpo bundles reviews with loyalty and referrals, and [Yotpo's pricing](https://www.yotpo.com/pricing/) scales with monthly order volume: Reviews has a limited free plan for up to 50 monthly orders and paid plans from $15/month. The limitation: Yotpo is expensive at scale and its breadth of features means the review product receives less focused development than single-purpose alternatives.

→ [Signed Reviews vs Yotpo](/vs/yotpo/)

### 5. Stamped.io. Best for Multi-Platform Mid-Market

Stamped.io offers solid multi-platform support at a mid-market price point, covering Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento, and WooCommerce under one roof. It combines reviews, ratings, Q&A, and loyalty features with purchase verification through order matching, making it a good all-rounder for brands that don't want to be locked into a single ecommerce platform. Its [Shopify app](https://apps.shopify.com/product-reviews-addon) starts at $23/month for up to 200 orders; plans bought directly start at [$199/month for Reviews](https://stamped.io/pricing). The limitation: Stamped.io's feature breadth means individual components (reviews, loyalty, Q&A) are less polished than dedicated best-in-class tools. It's a jack-of-all-trades, master of none.

→ [Signed Reviews vs Stamped](/vs/stamped/)

### 6. Okendo. Best for Visual-First Shopify DTC

Okendo is the premium Shopify review app for brands that treat reviews as a marketing asset: offering photo reviews, video testimonials, and customer attributes (skin type, size, fit) that make social proof more relevant to shoppers. Its [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/okendo-reviews) shows a free plan for up to 50 orders/month and a $19/month plan for up to 200 orders. Verification is standard Level 3 against Shopify order data. The limitation: Okendo is Shopify-exclusive, and above 200 orders it costs more than Judge.me, which caps at $15/month. You're paying for the visual polish and customer attribute data, which only pays off if you actively use those features in your marketing.

→ [Signed Reviews vs Okendo](/vs/okendo/)

### 7. G2. Best for B2B and Software Reviews

G2 is the dominant B2B software review platform. Reviewers must [sign up with a LinkedIn profile or a business email](https://documentation.g2.com/help/docs/verifying-your-account-with-linkedin), a different model than ecommerce purchase verification but credible in its own domain. For ecommerce brands selling SaaS, logistics software, or marketing tools alongside physical products, G2 captures the B2B buyer audience that Trustpilot doesn't reach. Its [free listing tier](https://sell.g2.com/plans) makes it accessible, with paid plans for more profile and marketing features. The limitation: G2's verification model doesn't confirm purchases at all. It confirms employment. For product reviews and consumer trust, G2 is the wrong tool; for B2B credibility and software category presence, it's essential.

## How to Switch from Trustpilot Without Losing Reviews

Switching review platforms midstream feels risky, but it's more straightforward than most merchants expect. Here's the step-by-step process:

**1. Save a copy of your Trustpilot reviews.** Check what export or API access your Trustpilot plan includes (API access is an add-on on some tiers), and keep the review text, star ratings, reviewer names, and dates. Trustpilot says each consumer owns their own reviews, so treat any copy you move as imported, not native.

**2. Import reviews into your new platform.** Some platforms covered here support CSV import, and Judge.me lists review import on its pricing page. Signed Reviews does not import reviews from other platforms: every review on it is matched to a Stripe charge, so plan to collect fresh ones.

**3. Run both platforms in parallel.** Get your new review platform configured, widget embedded, and automatic invitations flowing before you disconnect Trustpilot. Run both for 2-4 weeks while your new reviews accumulate. Once you have a fresh base of verified reviews on the new platform, remove the Trustpilot widget.

**4. Update your Google seller ratings.** If you use Google's seller rating extension, update the review feed URL to point to your new platform. Check that your new platform supports Google Shopping integration. Signed Reviews does not feed Google Seller Ratings today.

**5. Communicate the upgrade to customers.** Let your customers know you've switched to a platform with stronger verification. "We moved our reviews to [platform] so every rating you see is backed by a verified purchase". This is a trust-building moment, not an apology.

## What About Google Reviews?

Google Reviews are essential for e-commerce SEO, star ratings in search results directly impact click-through rates. But Google Reviews carry **zero purchase verification** (Level 0). Anyone with a Google account can leave a review. They're a distribution channel, not a verification strategy.

The optimal setup for most e-commerce brands: a verified review platform (Level 3 or 4) for your website + Google Reviews for search visibility. Use verified reviews as the authenticity signal; use Google as the distribution channel.

## How to Choose a Trustpilot Alternative for Your Store

### Verification vs. Open Reviews

The single biggest decision is whether you need purchase-verified reviews or can accept an open review model. Open platforms like Trustpilot and G2 let anyone with an account leave a review, great for volume, terrible for trust. Verified platforms confirm a transaction occurred before allowing a review, and the verification source matters: store-order verification (Judge.me, Yotpo) is solid, while processor-level verification (Signed Reviews, via Stripe) rests on charge records that come from Stripe, not from your store. If your brand competes on trust, and every ecommerce brand does, choose verified. For what each level actually proves, see [verified vs unverified reviews](/blog/verified-reviews-vs-unverified-reviews/).

### Pricing That Scales With Order Volume

Trustpilot alternatives span from free (Judge.me) to enterprise-quote territory (Yotpo), but the real cost isn't the sticker price. It's how pricing scales with your order volume. Platforms that charge per review or per invitation become unpredictable as you grow. Flat caps help (Judge.me tops out at $15/month), but most tiers here are priced by order volume (Loox adds $50 per extra 300 orders), so read the tier limits. Before committing, project your costs at 2x and 5x your current monthly order volume, the cheapest platform at 100 orders/month is rarely the cheapest at 1,000.

### Integration With Shopify, WooCommerce, and Stripe

Your review platform should plug into your existing stack without custom development. Shopify-native apps (Judge.me, Loox, Okendo) offer one-click installs but lock you into the Shopify ecosystem. Multi-platform tools (Stamped.io, Yotpo) give you portability at the cost of less deep integration. Stripe-native platforms (Signed Reviews) work across any ecommerce stack that takes payments through your own Stripe account (WooCommerce + Stripe, custom headless builds, a Shopify store with its own Stripe account; Shopify Payments stores can't connect) giving you both flexibility and the strongest verification signal. Match the integration depth to your growth trajectory: if you might replatform in the next two years, avoid Shopify-exclusive tools. Setup specifics for the three stacks we're asked about most: [Shopify](/integrations/shopify/), [WooCommerce](/integrations/woocommerce/), and [Stripe](/integrations/stripe/).

## Bottom Line

The best trustpilot alternatives for ecommerce fall into two camps: those that verify against your store data (Level 3, Shopify-native apps like Judge.me and Yotpo) and those that verify against your payment processor (Level 4, Stripe-native platforms like Signed Reviews). The Level 3 options are mature, feature-rich, and well-integrated. The Level 4 option checks each review against charge records from your payment processor and signs it, so it can't be gamed from your store admin.

For most e-commerce brands, the decision comes down to one question: does your store process payments through Stripe? If yes, you have access to processor-attested verification, a stronger trust signal than anything Trustpilot or the Shopify-native apps can offer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Trustpilot free for ecommerce stores?

Trustpilot has a free plan with 50 automated review invitations a month. Paid plans start at $99/month (Starter, only for new small businesses up to $5M revenue, 100 invitations) and $319/month (Plus, 300 invitations), billed annually on a 12-month contract. Product reviews are sold as a separate paid add-on. For a store sending more than 50 invitations a month, the free tier runs out fast.

### What is the cheapest Trustpilot alternative?

Judge.me is the cheapest: $0/month with unlimited reviews on Shopify, and $15/month at most. Loox has a free plan for up to 500 orders, then $49.99/month. Signed Reviews has a free plan and paid plans from $29/month, with each review checked against a completed Stripe charge. All three cost less than Trustpilot's $99/month Starter plan, and each ties reviews to order or payment data rather than invitations alone.

### Can I import my Trustpilot reviews?

Usually, yes. Check what export or API access your Trustpilot plan includes before you switch. Several alternative platforms (including Judge.me) support review import with tools that preserve your review history, star ratings, and dates. Signed Reviews does not import reviews, because each one must match a Stripe charge. Imported reviews are typically labeled as "imported" to distinguish them from natively verified reviews, preserving your social proof while being transparent about provenance. See the switching guide above for a step-by-step walkthrough.

### Do review widgets slow down my store?

They can, if a widget blocks the initial render. The key factor is whether the platform loads reviews via async JavaScript (fast, non-blocking) or server-side rendering that blocks the initial paint. To be safe, test widget performance in Google PageSpeed Insights after installation.
