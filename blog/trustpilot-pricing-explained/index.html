Trustpilot Pricing 2026: Plans, Real Costs & Cheaper Alternatives

What's on the Trustpilot pricing page: every 2026 plan from Free to Premium, add-ons, contract terms, real yearly costs and cheaper alternatives.

By Robinson Guerra, Founder · Published 2026-07-24 · Updated 2026-10-09

Trustpilot pricing in 2026 starts with a Free plan that sends 50 review invitations a month. Paid plans start at $99 (Starter), $319 (Plus) and $799 (Premium) per month, per domain, billed annually, and Enterprise is quote-only (Trustpilot pricing page). Every paid contract runs 12 months, prepaid. This article covers what each plan includes, what the "from" prices leave out, and where the extra costs sit.

What's actually on the Trustpilot pricing page

If you visit business.trustpilot.com/pricing, here's what you'll see, and what you won't:

  • Three published starting prices. Starter from $99, Plus from $319 and Premium from $799 per month, per domain, billed annually. Enterprise says "Contact sales for a pricing estimate."
  • A free plan with a small invitation allowance. Free includes 50 automated invitations a month. Starter gets 100, Plus 300, Premium 1,000 and Enterprise unlimited.
  • "From" prices, not final prices. The page says you'll get a personalized quote that considers site traffic, business size per domain and annual revenue. Each domain is priced separately.
  • What's missing: the Enterprise price and the prices of add-ons such as Product Reviews, Location Reviews and API access. Those emerge in the sales conversation.

That gap between the pricing page and the real bill is what the rest of this article fills in.

Trustpilot pricing: how the pricing model works

Trustpilot charges by plan tier and by domain (not by review volume directly), but higher tiers allow higher invitation volumes. There's no per-review pricing. You pay a fixed amount per domain for a bundle of features, and your review volume is capped by your tier's invitation limit.

Key things to understand before the numbers:

  • Most features are bundled into tiers. A few are sold as add-ons, but if you want a bundled feature from a higher tier, you pay for the whole tier.
  • Contracts run 12 months. The page says all contracts are a 12-month commitment and the initial term is prepaid. The listed prices are the billed-annually prices.
  • Invitations are capped by tier. 50, 100, 300 and 1,000 a month on Free, Starter, Plus and Premium. Only Enterprise is unlimited.
  • Add-ons cost extra. Product Reviews and Location Reviews are add-ons on every paid plan. API access is an add-on on Premium and Enterprise only, and isn't offered on the cheaper plans.

What you actually get: A listing on Trustpilot.com that anyone can review, plus 50 invitations a month and a review-collection widget. You can respond publicly, which matters, but you can't show your rating on your own site with a showcase TrustBox, and 50 invitations runs out fast for a store with real order volume.

Who it's for: Businesses that want to exist on Trustpilot, respond to reviews as they come in, and test invitations at low volume. A reputation-monitoring tool more than a collection strategy.

The catch: Organic reviews skew negative. People who had a bad experience seek out Trustpilot to complain; happy customers rarely do. Invitations balance the ratio by prompting satisfied customers, and the Free plan caps them at 50 a month. Past that, your Trustpilot profile will disproportionately reflect negative experiences. Trustpilot knows this. It's the incentive to upgrade.

Hidden and additional costs

Beyond the plan price, these cost Trustpilot users real money:

Is Trustpilot worth the price?

For some businesses, yes, particularly those where Trustpilot is a primary consumer discovery channel and a four-figure yearly bill is manageable. Trustpilot's brand recognition is real, and for certain industries (financial services, insurance, telecom), being absent from Trustpilot is a red flag. For a broader look at the platform's trustworthiness, see our assessment of whether Trustpilot is legit.

For most small and mid-size businesses, the math is harder to justify:

  • $319/month = $3,828/year per domain on Plus, for 300 invitations a month, on a platform where unverified reviews sit next to invited ones.
  • Verification is Level 3 (merchant-supplied). Trustpilot's "Verified" badge means the business invited the reviewer, not that a payment was independently confirmed. You're paying for invitations, not independent verification.
  • You don't own the reviews. Trustpilot owns the platform, the profile, and the review data. If you stop paying, your reviews stay on your Trustpilot profile, per Trustpilot's own pricing FAQ, but you lose your paid plan's invitation volume and widgets.

Before committing to a Trustpilot annual contract, ask: is the Trustpilot brand and consumer directory worth $1,188 to $9,588 a year per domain to your business, or would that budget go further on a platform that ties each review to a completed Stripe charge, gives you review ownership, and has a lower starting price?

Bottom line

Trustpilot pricing starts at free with 50 invitations a month. Paid plans run from $99 to $799 a month per domain on 12-month prepaid contracts, and the extras (Product Reviews, Location Reviews, API access, more domains) mean the real cost is often higher. The free plan is a reputation-monitoring tool, not a full review-collection one. Before signing an annual contract, understand exactly which features are in your tier, which are add-ons, and whether a Level 3 (merchant-supplied) verification model is the strongest signal you can offer your customers.
